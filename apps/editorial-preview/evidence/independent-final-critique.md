# Independent visual critique

Reviewed `final-1440-first.png`, `final-390-first.png`, `final-1440-proof.png`, and `final-390.png` against both current and donor first-screen captures. Static visual review only; no interaction or runtime claim.

## What works

- Substantially cleaner than the current site: consistent typography, restrained palette, useful contrast, aligned margins, and no intrusive floating circles over mobile content.
- The 390px hero fits comfortably, its principal CTA is visible, and section boundaries remain legible throughout the page. No obvious horizontal clipping appears in the supplied captures.
- The desktop FYD example is the strongest section: business selection, service selection, and source disclosure have a clear hierarchy. Lime marks the active item consistently.

## Concrete remaining weaknesses

1. **Desktop hero spends too much attention on the name.** The giant PING mark occupies roughly 430px vertically while the explanation is a small paragraph in the opposite corner. Visitors get a memorable masthead, but little evidence of the product in the first screen. The donor establishes a proposition and exposes the UI together; this candidate delays that combination.
2. **Mobile product evidence arrives late.** The full capture puts the useful FYD example after the hero, an extensive knowledge map/product record, and two conceptual panels. The early graph asks visitors to understand PING, PING Social, AI Concierge, and TenantOS before showing an ordinary business example. That is a large conceptual burden for a cold visitor.
3. **The naming hierarchy is confusing.** The map contains a business named PING Social and another product named PING Social, plus PING and AI Concierge. The later FYD example switches to Coppersmith and Happy Place. These may reflect correct records, but the presentation does not explain the relationship between the brands or why a visitor should choose one.
4. **Heading tracking is overly tight.** The global `letter-spacing: -.055em` makes several mobile headlines look squeezed, especially “Your business, understood,” “Good answers need something to stand on,” and “Start with the business you have.” A modest relaxation would look more finished and improve scanning without changing the visual direction.
5. **Secondary text gets unnecessarily tiny on mobile.** Graph type labels are 9px, graph names 11px, and FYD provenance/readonly footnotes 10px. Source transparency is part of this site's argument, so its explanatory text should not be among the hardest text to read.
6. **The FYD preview has competing exits.** “Explore the business and Ask FYD” inside the panel and “Explore FYD and Ask” immediately below it look like overlapping next steps. The section could communicate one primary exit more decisively.
7. **The long mobile page loses visual variety.** After the distinctive dark map, many sections repeat the same left-aligned label/headline/paragraph/rule/link pattern. It is coherent, but begins to resemble a generic editorial template. The donor has more distinctive typography and varied product composition; the candidate is less decorative but also less characteristic.

## Assessment

The candidate is a credible improvement over the current site and does not look broken or amateur overall. It is not yet a clear winner over the donor in product communication or visual character. Prioritize the path to the real business example, brand hierarchy, and heading spacing before adding decoration. No source files or runtime state were changed for this review.
